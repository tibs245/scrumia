# Review guard-rails: legal

## Invented certainty

- An obligation is stated as settled that nobody has verified. This is the defect
  that matters most here: a confident wrong answer stops anyone from asking.
- A regulation is named by acronym alone — "GDPR applies" — with no article,
  principle or specific requirement, so nobody can act on it.
- A legal basis is asserted with no reasoning a lawyer could check.
- An uncertainty is written as a hedge inside a conclusion instead of as an open
  question with what would settle it.

## Completeness

- Data is listed with no legal basis, or a basis with no data.
- A retention period is given with no end behaviour — deleted, anonymised,
  archived?
- A right is granted that nobody can exercise: no path, no owner, no delay.
- Consent is required and nothing says when it is asked or what is shown.
- The feature plainly processes a category of data the file does not mention.

## Boundaries

- An engineering risk with no legal trigger is written here. That is `security.md`'s.
- A residual legal risk is accepted with a format of its own instead of the
  security angle's acceptance record. Two formats drift.
- The implementation of a right — the endpoint, the screen — is designed here. Here
  it is required; `tech.md` and `ux.md` build it.
- The file exists because the feature felt sensitive, with no activation question
  actually answering yes.

## Hygiene

- A ticket, issue or PR number appears.
- A former obligation is kept alongside the current one "for context".
